Following AEW Dynamite and Collision Without Fake Scoops

Where official match information ends and predictions begin.

WRESTLEWIRE EDITORIAL · 22 November 2026 · ORIGINAL EXPLAINER

AEW's own schedule, event announcements and official results provide a foundation for covering weekly television. They help establish the existence of a show, the matches a promotion has announced and the results it chooses to publish.

An announced card is not necessarily a guarantee that every bout will happen exactly as advertised. Wrestling promotions can change plans. If a writer discusses possible outcomes or future rivalries, the correct label is preview, analysis or prediction rather than a confirmed result.

Professional wrestling fans also follow contracts, signings and backstage reports. Those topics can produce some of the biggest online headlines, but they require extra verification. An intriguing social-media post should not be presented as proof of a contract or release.

WrestleWire's AEW coverage will pair attributed official updates with original context. A regular source monitor can discover newly published official videos at no AI-API cost, while longer reported articles need separate editorial verification.
